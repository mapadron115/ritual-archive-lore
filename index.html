II · The Four-Kings Square

Four ways to rule
a world in motion.

Two wars nested inside one another: the metaphysical contest of Commitment and Emptiness, and the human contest of Order and Motion. None of the four believes himself evil. Each believes himself correct.

Commitment at the table

Kurosawa's cadence. Orders that make sense three moves late; a maxim borrowed mid-briefing; Enochian grammar that turns a question into compulsion. The party is asked to commit before it understands the board.

Emptiness at the table

The Empty Fort. Every clue in the arcade terminates at an absent center: three staged witnesses, a warm guqin, the minter positioned elsewhere. The party finds the apparatus, never the hand.

Order at the table

Mandates at the edge. Qin's audit is a pressure available across sessions; a mercury vial may fall into the wrong hands. The chapter's clocks, prices, permits, and ledgers make order tangible before Qin appears.

Motion at the table

Recruitment, not slaughter. Genghis may pressure the chapter by recruiting the PCs instead of killing them. His riders remain at the map's edge while the party decides which road to take — ash or completer, raid or grammar-break.
